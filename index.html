Reentry

This heart is not an animation. Each frame, your graphics chip solves the voltage of 25,000 patches of muscle.

The ECG is not drawn either. It is what an electrode would read off that voltage.

So it can be broken. Try.

What is real here

A 2D four-chamber slice runs the Aliev–Panfilov equations, a two-variable reduced model of cardiac action potentials, on a 256 × 256 grid. Waves, block, re-entry and fibrillation all emerge from those equations. None of them is scripted.

Computed, not drawn

  • The ECG. Each lead is a pseudo-ECG integrated from the voltage gradients, using Wilson's central terminal for the V leads. P, QRS and T come out of that sum.
  • The T wave, which is upright because the epicardium repolarises before the endocardium.
  • The irregularly irregular pulse of AF, which is the AV node filtering chaos.
  • Loss of pulse in VF. The muscle is still electrically busy, but it no longer contracts together.

Simplified, on purpose

  • A flat slice is not a heart. Real VF lives in 3D.
  • The cardiac wavelength is shrunk under ischaemia more than real tissue would, so re-entry fits a 2D slice. The direction is correct, the size is exaggerated.
  • Conduction velocity is slower than the real value relative to heart size, so the QRS is on the wide side of normal.
  • The AV node and Purkinje network are timed rules, not tissue. In AF, the node's concealed conduction includes some randomness.
  • AF here has few wavelets: a slice this small cannot hold real AF, which runs near 400 cycles a minute. Treat it as the mechanism, not the rate.
  • Shock always succeeds. Real defibrillation can fail, and the outcome depends on energy and on the substrate.
  • Blood pressure is an illustrative Windkessel estimate, calibrated to read near 120/80. It is not a haemodynamic model.
